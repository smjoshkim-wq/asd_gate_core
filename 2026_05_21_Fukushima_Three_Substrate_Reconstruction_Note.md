## 2. Incident Summary

Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Plant is a Boiling Water Reactor (BWR) facility on the Pacific coast of Fukushima Prefecture, Japan, operated by Tokyo Electric Power Company (TEPCO). Six units were in service at the time of the accident; Units 1, 2, and 3 were operating, and Units 4, 5, and 6 were in maintenance shutdown.

At 14:46 JST on March 11, 2011, the Tohoku M9.0 earthquake struck approximately 130 km offshore. All three operating units automatically SCRAMed per design. Diesel generators came online for cooling. At approximately 15:37 JST, the tsunami generated by the earthquake arrived at the Daiichi site with a peak height of approximately 14–15 m, overtopping the OP+5.7 m seawall, flooding the diesel generator rooms, and initiating a Station Blackout on Units 1–4. Battery power continued for some units for limited duration.

Over the following four days, the loss of cooling produced fuel damage, hydrogen generation, and three reactor building explosions (Unit 1 on March 12 at 15:36 JST; Unit 3 on March 14 at 11:01 JST; Unit 4 / Unit 2 containment damage on March 15 at 06:14 JST). Offsite radiation release prompted evacuation of approximately 160,000 residents from the surrounding area. Itate Village, located outside the initial 20 km evacuation zone but in the radiation plume path that SPEEDI predictions had identified by March 12, was not evacuated until April 22, 2011.

The accident has been investigated by the National Diet of Japan Independent Investigation Commission (NAIIC, 2012), the Government of Japan Investigation Committee (ICANPS, 2012), the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA, 2015), and multiple internal TEPCO and NRC reviews. The structural failures documented by these sources — TEPCO's pre-incident dismissal of higher tsunami probability estimates, the disordered operational chain of command during the response, and the parallel-command-structure failure in the offsite response — are the structural commissions this reconstruction maps to the three substrates.

### 3.1 Nuclear Substrate (Direct 1:1) — TEPCO HQ Seawater Injection Countermand

Compiler: `nuclear_compiler_v0_1.py`. Same compiler used in the Challenger reconstruction. Six action classes (N1 Monitor, N2 ReactivityControl, N3 ProtectiveMitigation, N4 EmergencyDeclaration, N5 ExternalNotification, N6 ExtremeOverride), four roles (RO, SRO_SM, ED, STA).

The reconstruction captures the operational sequence at Fukushima Daiichi Unit 1 from the routine pre-earthquake monitoring (March 11, 14:46 JST) through the seawater injection countermand episode (March 12, ~20:00 JST). The structural violation occurs when a TEPCO HQ actor (Executive Officer Takekuro, conveying PM Kan office concerns about recriticality) enters the Site Superintendent's emergency response workflow and issues a directive to throttle the seawater injection that Yoshida had initiated.

The action mapping: `throttle_eccs` → N6_ExtremeOverride. The role mapping: HQ_Takekuro defaults to STA (he was a corporate HQ Fellow, not a licensed SRO_SM at the Daiichi site, and is therefore not in the compiler's `NUCLEAR_ROLE_TABLE`). Same `shift_id` as Yoshida's emergency response workflow. The actor_pivot detection mechanism fires EXIT.

Eight admissible steps precede the violation: ro_jones monitoring the pre-earthquake reactor state (steps 1–3, on a separate shift workflow), sro_chen taking operational direction post-tsunami on the emergency response workflow (steps 4–8: STANDBY → MONITORING → OPERATING → EMERGENCY_RESPONSE, with loop-back N3 actions for high-pressure injection and eventually seawater injection). The seawater injection action itself (step 8, `actuate_eccs`) is admissible — N3 in SRO_SM vocabulary, loop in EMERGENCY_RESPONSE. The structural violation is HQ's directive, not Yoshida's response.

Historical resolution: Yoshida disobeyed the directive and continued seawater injection. The gate's fire records the structural illegitimacy of HQ's directive, independent of whether the site complied.

Reconstruction type: Direct 1:1 (the action sequence maps directly to documented events with attributed roles and known timing from the Yoshida hearings and NAIIC).

Lead time precision class: Hour-level (the seawater countermand episode is documented to within an hour in the Yoshida hearings).

Lead time to consequence: 39.0 hours from the structural violation (20:00 JST March 12) to the Unit 3 hydrogen explosion (11:01 JST March 14). Yoshida's disobedience is widely credited with preventing further fuel damage on Unit 1; the lead time figure is not a counterfactual claim about whether the directive would have changed the Unit 3 outcome.

### 3.2 Org Workflow Substrate (Structural Analog) — TEPCO 2008 Risk Assessment Override

Compiler: `org_workflow_compiler_v0_1.py`. Same compiler used in the Challenger and Therac reconstructions. Five action classes (A1 Review, A2 Assess, A3 Recommend, A4 Authorize, A5 Execute), two roles (Analyst, Approver).

The reconstruction captures TEPCO's 2008 internal tsunami risk assessment and the management decision to dismiss its recommendation. The TEPCO Nuclear Power Division engineering team conducted a probabilistic tsunami hazard assessment in 2008, prompted by the 2002 Headquarters for Earthquake Research Promotion (HERP) seismic hazard map updates. The assessment estimated maximum credible tsunami height at 15.7 m at the Daiichi site — significantly above the OP+5.7 m seawall and the OP+10 m design basis. The engineering team formally recommended seawall upgrade or relocation of emergency diesel generators. Estimated cost: approximately $60M USD.

The Nuclear Power Division management subsequently issued an internal counter-recommendation declining the upgrade. The decision was framed as "not academic consensus" and "not regulator-mandated," with cost cited as a factor. The recommendation was shelved as "for future study."

The structural violation: A management actor (mapped as Approver) enters the engineering team's recommendation workflow and issues `recommend_approval` (A3_Recommend) — an action class that is structurally Analyst-only. Same `workflow_id` as the engineering team's recommendation. The actor_pivot detection mechanism fires EXIT.

Six admissible steps precede the violation: analyst_alice reviewing HERP documentation, reading prior assessments, conducting the probabilistic analysis, evaluating it against design basis, formally recommending the upgrade, and flagging the concern (steps 1–6: IDLE → REVIEWING → ASSESSING → RECOMMENDING). The structural violation is the management override (step 7).

This is structurally identical to the Challenger Mason "management hat" moment and to the Therac AECL response pattern. The same compiler, the same role boundary, the same action-class crossing. The third instance of this exact geometry in the reconstruction corpus.

Reconstruction type: Structural analog (the compiler was built for generic decision pipelines; the TEPCO 2008 episode is mapped to its vocabulary).

Lead time precision class: Month-level (NAIIC documents the 2008 decisions to the month; specific day not always documented in the public sources).

Lead time to consequence: 915 days (~2.5 years) from the structural violation (September 2008) to the tsunami arrival at Daiichi (March 11, 2011). This is the longest lead time in the project's reconstruction corpus, exceeding the Gelsinger pharma case (4 days), the Therac series (18 months end-to-end), and all of the operational-substrate reconstructions.

### 3.3 FEMA ICS Substrate (Structural Analog) — PM Kan Office Invasion of LNERH IC Workflow

Compiler: `fema_compiler_v0_1.py`. Same compiler used in the Deepwater Horizon reconstruction. Seven action classes (AC1 Assessment, AC2 Planning, AC3 ResourceOrder, AC4 Execution, AC5 CommandTransfer, AC6 PublicComm, AC7 Demobilization), three roles (IC, OSC, Field_Resource).

The reconstruction captures the offsite emergency response coordination failure during March 11–12, 2011. Under the Japanese Nuclear Emergency Preparedness Act, the Local Nuclear Emergency Response Headquarters (LNERH) at the Off-Site Center, run by NISA officials, was the designated Incident Commander equivalent for offsite nuclear emergency response. The LNERH was formally activated on the evening of March 11 and began conducting situational assessment, planning, and unified command activation per the doctrine in force.

PM Kan's office subsequently announced an evacuation order (2 km Daiichi / 3 km Daini) directly to the public via national press conference at 21:23 JST on March 11, bypassing the LNERH chain of communication. This was the first of four PM-office-issued evacuation announcements that structurally bypassed the LNERH IC chain (subsequent: 10 km on March 12 at 05:44 JST, 20 km on March 12 at 18:25 JST, and later expansions).

The structural violation: PM Kan's office (mapped as Field_Resource by default — not in the compiler's `FEMA_ROLE_TABLE`; the PM's office is not designated as IC under the Nuclear Emergency Preparedness Act) enters the LNERH IC's incident workflow and issues `issue_public_warning` (AC6_PublicComm) — an action class that is structurally IC-only. Same `incident_id` as LNERH's IC workflow. The actor_pivot detection mechanism fires EXIT.

Six admissible steps precede the violation: ic_thompson (LNERH director) conducting size-up, assessing resources, drafting objectives, conducting the planning meeting, activating unified command, and approving the IAP (steps 1–6: STANDBY → ASSESSMENT → PLANNING → UNIFIED_COMMAND). The structural violation is the PM office's invasion of this workflow (step 7).

This is structurally analogous to the BP `issue_public_warning` pattern in the Deepwater Horizon FEMA reconstruction — a non-IC actor attempting public communications on a designated IC's incident response. The same compiler, the same actor_pivot mechanism, applied to a different incident.

Reconstruction type: Structural analog (the compiler was built for FEMA NIMS/ICS doctrine; the Japanese Nuclear Emergency Preparedness Act is mapped to its vocabulary on the structural correspondence that both doctrines designate a single offsite IC equivalent).

Lead time precision class: Minute-level (PM Kan office announcements are documented to the minute via press conference records).

Lead time to consequence: 43 days from the structural violation (21:23 JST March 11) to the Itate Village evacuation (April 22, 2011). Itate is the documented late-evacuation case in the offsite response corpus; the SPEEDI radiation projection data was withheld from the village during the intervening six weeks. Note that the SPEEDI withholding is an omission and is not detected (R5 passive failure boundary).

## 4. The External-Trigger Robustness Finding — A New Empirical Property

This reconstruction introduces a result that none of the prior reconstructions could test: substrate-invariance composition against an externally-precipitated cascade.

The Tohoku M9.0 earthquake and tsunami of March 11, 2011, are the precipitating events for the Fukushima Daiichi accident. Both are fully external to any human decision system the framework's compilers cover. The gate cannot detect external precipitating events — it operates on structured action sequences committed by human actors.

The empirical question this poses: when the initiating event is external, does the gate still fire on the human actor commissions that follow?

The answer from the three substrates: yes, all three.

The nuclear substrate fires on the TEPCO HQ seawater countermand, 29 hours after the tsunami and 39 hours before the worst downstream consequence (Unit 3 explosion). The fire is independent of the external trigger — the gate detects HQ's structural commission, not the tsunami.

The org workflow substrate fires on the TEPCO 2008 management override, approximately 2.5 years BEFORE the external trigger. The structural vulnerability the cascade would later exploit was committed in the decision pipeline before the precipitating event existed. The gate's detection is fully independent of any external trigger; the trigger merely turned a pre-existing structural vulnerability into a catastrophic cascade.

The FEMA ICS substrate fires on the PM Kan office's invasion of the LNERH IC workflow, approximately 6 hours after the tsunami. The fire is independent of the external trigger — the gate detects the parallel-command-structure violation, not the radiation release.

The combined finding: external precipitation does not preclude downstream structural detection. The gate's selectivity property (it detects structural commissions, not external events) is preserved when the external event is the initiating cause of the cascade rather than a downstream consequence. The substrate-invariance composition claim survives this falsification attempt.

A secondary observation: the lead times for the three substrate fires span four orders of magnitude (minute-level, hour-level, year-level). The framework does not have a characteristic timescale. It detects geometric violations whenever they are committed, regardless of when the consequence will manifest.

## 5. The Within-Event Structural-Geometry Recurrence Finding

Across the three substrates of this reconstruction, the same structural geometry fires: an actor not authorized for a workflow enters that workflow and attempts an action class outside their role's vocabulary. The compiler-level mechanism that detects this is actor_pivot (a different identity entering an existing workflow_id / shift_id / incident_id), which surfaces as EXIT in the gate output.

The three instances:

| Substrate | Authorized actor (workflow owner) | Invading actor | Action class attempted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nuclear | SRO_SM (Yoshida, Site Superintendent) | STA (HQ_Takekuro, corporate HQ) | N6_ExtremeOverride (`throttle_eccs`) |
| Org Workflow | Analyst (engineering team) | Approver (Nuclear Power Division management) | A3_Recommend (`recommend_approval` as counter-recommendation) |
| FEMA ICS | IC (LNERH, NISA Off-Site Center) | Field_Resource (PM Kan office, default mapping) | AC6_PublicComm (`issue_public_warning`) |

Three different substrates. Three different action class vocabularies. Three different role registries. Three different workflow / shift / incident identifier mechanisms. One identical structural geometry across all three. One identical gate fire across all three.

This is the within-event version of the cross-substrate finding documented across the project. It demonstrates that the same incident exhibited the same structural geometry at three independent layers — operational, decision pipeline, and offsite response coordination. The structural failure mode at Fukushima was not three separate failure modes; it was one failure mode (management invasion of operational workflows) recurring at three different organizational scales.

For the substrate-invariance claim, this is a strengthening result. The same geometry, the same compiler-detected mechanism, three substrates, one event. The cross-substrate generalization documented across the project (the same invariants applying across domains) is mirrored by a within-event generalization (the same geometry applying across organizational layers within a single incident).

Was the cascade preventable? The gate does not address this question. It identifies WHERE the structural geometry was violated and WHEN. The TEPCO 2008 management override could have been rejected by an alternative management decision; the seawall upgrade could have been performed; the cascade vulnerability could have been removed. None of that is a counterfactual claim the framework supports. The framework records what the geometry looked like at the moment of each commission.

It was not, on the substrates where the geometry was committed. The gate fires.
